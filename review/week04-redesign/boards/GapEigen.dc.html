Does it matter how central a job's neighbours are?

Somewhat. Eigenvector centrality and PageRank agree at Spearman 0.71: eigenvector rewards the dense software core, PageRank lifts small, tight clusters.

A job scores high when the jobs it links to score high. Start every score at 1, replace each with the weighted sum of its neighbours, rescale, repeat. scores a job by its neighbours' scores. PageRank splits each job's vote among its links and adds random jumps, so a link from a job with few partners counts for more. On this undirected network PageRank tracks strength closely (Spearman 0.95).

💡What to notice The three school-teacher jobs have 14 to 17 partners each, mostly one another. Eigenvector centrality ranks them 271th to 278th of 289, because their neighbours are not central. PageRank puts them 25th to 32th. Managers and HR specialists, tied into the software core, fall the other way.
Method

The same 289-occupation backbone PageRank runs on (α = 0.05, 2,984 links), weighted by shared companies. networkx's eigenvector_centrality_numpy; PageRank at d = 0.85 as in the PageRank box.

More numbers

Spearman with degree: eigenvector 0.84, PageRank 0.93. With strength: eigenvector 0.87, PageRank 0.95. The two share 10 of their top 15.
