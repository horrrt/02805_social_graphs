B

Which jobs belong to two clusters?

Only 5 of 393 pass, fewer than the 19 a test this strict passes by chance, so no occupation clearly belongs to two clusters.

We looked for occupations that also belong to a second cluster, with more employer ties there than any rewired network gives them.

How the test worksAn occupation’s second cluster is the one its employer ties exceed most over the expectation modularity uses (its strength times the cluster’s, over twice the total weight). A ratio above 1, our first rule, marks 145 occupations, but the rewired networks mark 452 on average with the same cluster labels. So an occupation now counts only when its ratio beats its own ratio in every rewired network.

C

Do the clusters follow official job groups?

The clusters follow the official groups only in part: NMI 0.23 against 0.05 for shuffled labels (AMI 0.19), over the 438 occupations in clusters of two or more.

The government groups occupations by their first two SOC digits. We compare those labels with the clusters found from hiring patterns.

What to notice. The software cluster also holds engineers, accountants and managers: companies hire across the official groups.

How the comparison worksThe shuffled bars keep the clusters fixed and scramble only the official labels. We keep certified H-1B filings and identify companies by tax number, as in the other sections. A link counts the companies that filed for both occupations. Filings still on 2010 computer codes (48 filings) move to their 2018 successors. Louvain runs 100 times on the full projection and the best modularity run is kept; the runs agree at a median NMI of 0.99. The null rewires the company × occupation network 20 times, keeping each company’s number of occupations and each occupation’s number of companies, and projects it again; real and rewired networks are scored on their largest connected piece (z = 285).Backbone, FY2024 and InfomapThe disparity filter at α = 0.2, as in the place section, keeps 7,639 of 28,158 links and 345 occupations. Louvain on that backbone finds 8 clusters, which match the full network’s at NMI 0.69, against 1.00 between two runs on the full network: the clusters only partly survive the filter. NMI and AMI leave out occupations alone in a cluster and are compared with 100 shuffles of the major-group labels. The same method on FY2024 gives clusters that match FY2025 at NMI 0.59 on the 388 occupations both years share. Infomap, the random-walk method, finds 9 clusters of two or more occupations; they agree with Louvain’s at NMI 0.75 and match the official groups at 0.25.
