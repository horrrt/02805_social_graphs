Layout system: how every section and card is built

One set of rules for the Week 4 page. Every board on Start here follows them; when a card breaks one, fix the card, not the rule. Each figure earns its place by answering the card's question.

A section

Read top to bottom: the claim, then the one card that shows it, then the questions that test it.

1Opener. Big section number, title, one-sentence finding.
2Intro card. How the network is built, plus the section's headline chart.
3Start card. The first question a reader asks, answered with one chart.
4Question cards 1A, 1B… Each tests the finding one way. Same anatomy, same order.
  1. 1
    The opener carries the finding.A reader who stops here still leaves with the point.
  2. 2
    At most one intro card and one start card.Anything else is a question card with a letter.
  3. 3
    Question cards run in order of doubt.Easiest objection first, the method checks last.
  4. 4
    Deep-dive topics use the same card.Numbered 1, 2, 3 in contents order, no letter codes.

A card

One chart: words on the left (5 of 12), the chart on the right (7 of 12). Two charts: the lead beside the notice, both charts in one row under them.

AHeader: badge (Start, 1A…), the question, a bold one-line answer.
BLead. Two sentences: what was measured, what it shows.
CWhat to notice. One comparison with its number.
▸ Background▸ Method▸ More numbers▸ Table: …
D · drawer row, always last in this column
EFigure title states what to look for, then a one-line note on how to read it.
Fone primary figure
GTwo charts. The lead and the notice share the top row; both charts sit side by side under them at equal width, titles and charts level; the drawers follow the row.
  1. A
    The answer fits on one line.Under about 12 words; the lead explains it.
  2. F
    One chart beside the words, two in a row.Never a chart inside the text column, never a chart alone in a half-empty row.
  3. B
    No card without a lead.A chart never stands alone: the left column always says what was measured and what it shows.
  4. D
    Four drawer names, this order.Background, Method, More numbers, then Table: or Maps: named by content. All closed.

When to add a figure

Ask three questions. If any answer is no, leave the figure out or move it into a drawer.

  1. 1
    Does it show the card's answer?Not a related fact, the answer itself.
  2. 2
    Can a reader find the point in five seconds?Highlight what the answer names; everything else grey.
  3. 3
    Does it compare with something?A baseline, a previous year, the other group. A lone number is a sentence, not a chart.
Avoid
Decorative infographics, a chart that repeats the text, two charts of the same numbers, legends with more than five entries, categories like "Other labelled".

Pick the chart by the question

QuestionChart
Real vs chanceHorizontal bars or dots with whiskers; real in orange or navy, baselines grey, the fair baseline blue.
Who is largestRanked horizontal bars, names left, values right-aligned at the end.
Over timeLine or slope chart, years on x, 2026 dashed (October to June).
Share of a wholeOne stacked bar or a composition bar, at most five parts plus "Other".
WhereMap with the three group colours and direct city labels.
Who links to whomNetwork with direct labels, straight links, or a flow diagram.
Look-up detailA table in a Table: drawer, not a chart.

Colour means something

OrangePlaced at a client, outsourcing firms, the observed value.
BlueDirect employers, the fair baseline.
Accent navyThe thing the answer names, in single-series charts.
GreyBaselines, context, everything the answer does not name.
Violet, green, slateThe three metro groups. Maps and their legends only.
Navy rampRanked categories by size, largest darkest.

Labels and numbers

  1. 1
    Never cut a label with "…".Wrap to two lines, or shorten the name once and keep it in full on hover.
  2. 2
    Values sit beside the mark.At the bar end or past the whisker, never under it.
  3. 3
    Numbers right-aligned in tabular figures.Bars in a table start at one x position.
  4. 4
    Axis titles say what is counted, in words."Companies that filed for both jobs in 2025", not "weight".
  5. 5
    Years as 2025.2026 always carries "October to June".

Words

  1. 1
    Visible paragraphs: two sentences.Supporting detail goes in a drawer.
  2. 2
    First line says what to expect.In drawers, each paragraph opens with a bold one-line point.
  3. 3
    Jargon gets a dotted underline.Hover explains it in one or two plain sentences, on first use per board.
  4. 4
    Figure titles are claims or questions."Do outsourcers cluster jobs like random firms would?", not "NMI".
  5. 5
    No code names, file names or internal IDs.No .py files, no box codes like M4.

Space

  1. 1
    Columns end near the same height.If one column is twice the other, move a block across or go full width.
  2. 2
    Closed drawers take one row.An open drawer takes the full width under that row.
  3. 3
    Fixed rhythm.Card padding 24, column gap 36, 14 between blocks, 18 above a figure.
  4. 4
    One control style.The segmented control for up to five options; a dropdown beyond.
  5. 5
    Every board carries the dot rail.Hover opens one contents panel with plain names.
