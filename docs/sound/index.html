    What you are hearing

    Higher-degree articles play higher notes on a logarithmic scale. Each detected community receives one of four labelled synthetic voices, reused across groups. Silence at an isolate is the data speaking.

    Try this: start at Baymax. The walk plays its single starting note and stops: there is no link to follow.

    Sound is an encoding, not evidence by itself. The step table carries every article, degree, community and pitch. The same seed and settings produce the same walk.

    THE TAKEAWAY

    Local choices do not explore a network evenly.

    A walk can return to the same hubs repeatedly while other articles never get heard. In a connected undirected graph, the long-run random-walk visitation distribution is proportional to degree. A short melody is a demonstration, not proof that its observed frequencies have converged.

    The 17 isolates cannot be reached from elsewhere, and their walks stop after the starting note. Directed walks may stop at other articles with no outgoing links.

    What the data means

    303 article entries and 1,784 directed links from the course’s frozen 26 August 2026 snapshot. A link means one article links to another within this roster. It does not measure friendship, hero strength, popularity or the whole Marvel universe.

    Limits of the experiment

    The seed makes an example reproducible, not representative. Voice assignment and pitch mapping are design choices. Communities are one seeded Louvain partition. No autoplay, recording or external music is used. This is a free-play experiment, not a course post.

    Methods and reproducibility

    Each next step is sampled uniformly from the current article’s sorted neighbours using the seeded generator shared with the terminal. Pitch is MIDI 48 + round(36 × log(1 + undirected degree) / log(107)). Community modulo four selects sine, triangle, soft sawtooth or soft square voices. Audio is synthesized locally with Web Audio; WAV export uses the same schedule through OfflineAudioContext.

    Analysis and source code on GitHub. All experiments run locally in your browser; the frozen data is never edited.
