How we cut the hairball

Two metros are linked when a company files in both; the weight adds up, over those companies, the smaller of its two filing counts. One weight threshold would keep the links among the big hubs and cut a mid-size metro's strongest tie, which is light next to New York and Dallas. The disparity filter keeps a link when it carries an unusually large share of either endpoint's weight at level α, the method the course used for the philosophers backbone. Weights come from analysis/week04_where.py.

💡 What to notice The split is real but weak: modularity is 0.053 against 0.013 for rewired networks that keep each company's number of metros (z = 28), and all 50 Louvain runs find it. Do the same employers tie distant cities together?

(who leads the long links)

Mostly not. Big direct employers lead the long links, Amazon above all, and no single firm carries one. The shortlist is the five firms that place the most filings at client sites: Tata Consultancy Services, Cognizant, Infosys, HCL and Compunnel. The staffing section follows them to their clients.

Distance vs weight

Each point is a backbone edge. Size scales gently with weight. Each label names a company on its heaviest edge over 1,500 km, for the eight heaviest; hover any point for its own. Use the legend to separate the shortlist from other lead employers; click a point to select a city.

One employer’s map

Only the cities this firm links — no grey background clutter. Arcs animate along the route. Map is fixed; click a city to select it.

💡 What to notice Of the 84 backbone links longer than 1,500 km, the shortlist leads 24 (29%); it leads 49 of the 99 shorter ones (49%). Amazon leads the most long links (24), then Cognizant (18), EY and Deloitte. The leading company carries a median 12% of a long link's weight, and only one long link, San Jose to Fayetteville (Walmart), has a company with half of it. Distant metros are tied by many companies filing in both.

With filing counts or without?

Louvain on the firm–client network for FY2025, 100 runs each: once with links weighted by filings, once with every link counting one. The two partitions share an NMI of …, less than two runs of either kind with different seeds (… weighted, … unweighted), so the filing counts change the grouping.
