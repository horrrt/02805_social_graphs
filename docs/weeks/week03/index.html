              Methods, and what this cannot tell you

              Migration. UN DESA International Migrant Stock, revision 2024. A node is a country, an arc origin → destination carries the number of people born in the origin who live in the destination. It is a stock, so it counts who is there now, not who moved this year, and a difference between two years is not a flow.

              Betweenness. Computed on the weighted directed graph with distance = 1 / people, so a heavy corridor is a short step. On the unweighted matrix the top brokers come out as Australia, Norway and Denmark, which ranks statistical reporting systems rather than countries: register countries name hundreds of tiny origins while survey countries bucket them into "other".

              Flights. OpenFlights route data, aggregated from airport pairs to country pairs, counting distinct routes. It is a single undated snapshot, last substantially refreshed around 2014. It does not vary by year and is drawn identically at every point on the year slider. It measures whether a route exists, not seats, frequency or passengers. Its degree and betweenness are computed exactly as the migration side's are, with distance = 1 / routes, so the two series on the scatter are the same two quantities measured on two networks.

              Reporting coverage. DESA's table is ragged. The United States names 58 origin countries and Denmark names 202, because one source is a survey with an "other" bucket and the other is a population register. Every degree count on this page inherits that.
