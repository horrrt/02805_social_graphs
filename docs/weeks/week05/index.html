A Marvel search engine in 20 lines

Can bag-of-words find the right page from a description?

What we asked

If we treat a short description such as “Norse god of thunder” as a tiny document, turn every Wikipedia page into a Bag-of-Words vector, and rank the 303 pages by cosine similarity — how often does the right character come first?

What we did

Lowercased alphanumeric tokens, CountVectorizer over all 303 pages, cosine similarity on raw counts. Twelve fixed queries with a target page each. Baseline: a uniform guess among 303 pages (~0.33%). We also reran the same queries with English stopwords removed, to see how much of/with/a were steering the ranking. Script: analysis/week05_search.py.

    What surprised us

    Raw Bag-of-Words puts the right page first in only … queries. Dropping stopwords lifts top-5 hits to … — still far from perfect, but already better than chance (~0.33% at rank 1). The … raw failures are the point: common words drown the signal that TF-IDF will reweight next week.

    Limitation

    Cosine on raw counts treats every token equally, so of and with compete with adamantium. The method also needs the target page to exist: Thor, Loki and Iron Man are missing from this category snapshot, so even a perfect ranker cannot return them.

    Community autocomplete

    Does each Marvel community write in its own voice?

    What we asked

    If we train one trigram generator per Louvain community and ask it to invent a new character’s Wikipedia lead — can a reader tell which community wrote which page?

    What we did

    Louvain on the undirected weighted Marvel network (100 seeds; modularity …, most-frequent partition in … of runs, null z = …). Communities with at least eight pages each get a trigram model (P(w₃ | w₁, w₂) from their article text). One fake lead per community. Chance baseline: … with … pages. Script: analysis/week05_autocomplete.py.

      Loading fake pages…

      Read the nonsense carefully: names, teams and repeated bigrams often leak the community even when the sentences do not parse.

      What surprised us

      The fake pages are locally fluent and globally broken — classic trigram behaviour — yet each one still smells like its community (X-Men claws, street-level kung fu, symbiotes, mystic orders). Whether that smell is strong enough for other groups is the number we still owe; until then, try the quiz above against the 1-in-k chance line.

      Limitation

      Trigrams copy local Wikipedia habits (infobox leftovers, “created by…”, earth-number fragments) as eagerly as character voice. A correct guess may mean “this community’s boilerplate”, not a deeper narrative style. Tiny communities were excluded (size < 8), so the quiz only covers the large clusters.
