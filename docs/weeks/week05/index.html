The Marvel network gets language

Headline question and the one figure that answers it. Pick the strongest of the seven sections once the results are in.

Opening

What the data is: 303 English Wikipedia pages from Category:Marvel Comics superheroes, frozen 26 August 2026, and the links between them. What a link means, what a token is, and which question the post answers. Self-contained: no earlier post needed.

1

Turn links into relationships

Enemies reach across the network's communities and family stays inside them, even with a word list doing the reading.

What we asked

Do enemies sit in different communities from allies and family?

Yes. Enemy links cross between communities more often than chance, family links less often.

What we did

A link from A to B means A's Wikipedia page links to B's. For each of the 1,784 links we took the first sentence on A's page that names B, and found one for 1,568 (88%). The rest come from infoboxes and navigation boxes, which our copy of the text has lost.

A small word list labels each sentence: killed, family, enemy, ally or teammate. We blank out every character's name first and match lower-case words only, so Black Widow is not a widow. 877 of the sentences (56%) match no word at all.

The communities come from 100 Louvain runs on the link network. For each label we counted the share of its links whose two characters sit in different communities, and compared it with 1,000 shuffles of the labels over the 691 labelled links.

Method

Each page splits into paragraphs at its line breaks and into sentences with spaCy's rule-based sentencizer. Sentences under References, External links, Notes, See also and Further reading are dropped. A character is found by its page title without the disambiguation ("Storm (Marvel Comics)" is "Storm") and by the real name its description gives in brackets ("Emil Blonsky" for the Abomination), as a whole word.

A sentence that matches several labels takes the first of killed, family, enemy, ally, teammate. Negation is not handled. Louvain runs on the weighted, undirected link network with seeds 2805 to 2904; the runs disagree with each other, so every share is an average over all 100 of them.

More numbers

A stricter shuffle only swaps labels between links that leave the same page, so a page written in hostile words throughout cannot carry the result. Enemies still cross more than it predicts (46%, z = 3.2) and family less (34%, z = −2.9).

The labelled links: 226 teammate, 205 enemy, 115 family, 86 killed and 59 ally. 118 sentences match more than one label.

Figure

Links that join two communities, by label

Dot: the share of that label's links whose characters sit in different communities, averaged over 100 Louvain runs. Band: the same share when the labels are shuffled, mean and one standard deviation either side.

What surprised us

💡What to notice 54% of enemy links join two communities, against 42% when the labels are shuffled (z = 4.0). Family links cross only 25% of the time (z = −4.1). Allies sit in between at 30%; teammate and killed links look like shuffled ones.

Limitation

The word list reads the sentence, not the pair. Of the 60 labels we read, 30 describe how A and B relate. A long sentence that names B often tells of a third character: on Cyclops's page, "Jean … is killed shortly after in battle with Xorn". Enemy and ally labels hold up best (7 and 9 of 12), teammate worst (4 of 12).

What we checked in the text

The 60 sentences we read, 12 per label, drawn at random. ✓ means the label describes how A and B relate; ✗ says what the sentence is about instead.

2

Catch Wikipedia copying itself

Pages copy each other when they share a mantle or a family, and what they copy is the paperwork around a character more than its story.

What we asked

Which Marvel pages copy text from each other?

Pages about the same mantle or the same family: 22 pairs in 12 clusters, and 20 of those pairs already link to each other.

What we did

We split every page into lower-case words, 740,282 in all, and collected every run of eight words in a row, an 8-gram. A run found on many pages is house style, not copying: "in American comic books published by Marvel Comics" is on 282 of the 303 pages. So 8-grams on more than 10 pages are set aside.

The remaining 8-grams merge into passages, one long copied passage counting once. Two pages copy each other when they share a passage of 30 words or more. The copying network links those pages, and its connected pieces are the clusters.

Method

A word is a run of letters and digits, with an apostrophe inside a word kept ("jean's"); punctuation and line breaks are dropped. Each word keeps its place on the page, so every shared passage is quoted from the page itself. A passage is labelled with the section heading above it.

The check: a pair that copies is compared with all 45,753 pairs of pages and with the 2,830 pairs that share only a phrase, an 8-gram run shorter than 30 words. A link counts in either direction.

Table: the 12 clusters

Figure

The copying network

Each dot is a page, sized by the words it shares. A line joins two pages that share a passage of 30 words or more, thicker for more shared words; a dashed line means the two pages do not link to each other. Hover a line for the passage.

What surprised us

💡What to notice 20 of the 22 copying pairs link to each other, against 3.1% of all pairs of pages and 16% of pairs that share only a phrase. The copied words are paperwork: 39% sit under Publication history and 23% under In other media, and only 5% in the character's biography.

Limitation

The text has no edit history, so we cannot tell who copied whom. The cutoffs matter only at the edges: setting the house-style cutoff anywhere from 3 to 20 pages gives the same 22 pairs, but runs of 12 words add 16 pairs from one templated paragraph, the lead that eight Strikeforce: Morituri pages share.

What we checked in the text

The longest passage of the three largest copying pairs, and the templated lead the 12-word runs find, quoted from the first page of each pair.
